The early and mid-1990s: when the Macintosh line expanded... to an extreme.
Apple very nearly went bankrupt in 1997 because they had introduced so many models with negligible differences that their customers had become confused and sales had plummeted.
Also during this time, the Macintosh switched to a different CPU architecture for the first time: from Motorola 68000 to PowerPC.

The late 1990s: when a huge Mac revamp saved Apple.
In early 1997, co-founder Steve Jobs returned to Apple as "interim" CEO, and by early 1998, he had pared the Mac lineup down to just four models: consumer desktop, professional desktop, consumer laptop, and professional laptop. This greatly streamlined Apple's offerings and helped turn the company's fortunes around.
This is my personal favorite era of the Mac due to the whimsical, flashy designs offered at the time.
