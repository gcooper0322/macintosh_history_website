The iBook (retroactively the iBook G3) was introduced in July 1999 as Apple's consumer laptop offering. Intended to be a portable version of the iMac (and indeed marketed as "iMac to go"), the first variant of the iBook featured a 300MHz G3 CPU, no legacy I/O interfaces, a 12-inch display, 32MB of RAM, a 3.2GB or 6GB hard drive, a CD drive, and optional wireless networking card, packed into a unique clamshell-shaped case with a latchless display lid and integrated carrying handle, available in either "Blueberry" or "Tangerine" color schemes. It sold for $1,599.
The iBook was almost as successful as the iMac, and received several revisions. The first of these added three new colors (Graphite, Indigo, and Key Lime) and a FireWire 400 (IEEE 1394) port, as well as bumping the CPU speed to 366MHz and later 466MHz. In May 2001, a faster version was introduced that shipped in a smaller, lighter, all-white case, for a price of $1,299. The iBook G3 was succeeded by the iBook G4 in October 2003, and the iBook name was dropped with the transition to Intel CPUs in 2006.
