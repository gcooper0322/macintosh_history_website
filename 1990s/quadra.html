The Macintosh Quadra series was introduced in October 1991 as a replacement for the Macintosh II series at the high end of Apple's product lineup. The first two models, the Quadra 700 and 900, were packaged in tower-style desktop cases; the 700's case had the same dimensions as that of the Macintosh IIci but was oriented vertically, while the 900 had a completely new full-tower case design. Both of these initial models featured 25MHz Motorola 68040 CPUs and 4MB of RAM as standard. The 700 sold for $5,700, while the 900 sold for $7,200.

As always, these first models were succeeded by newer, faster ones. All models featured the 68040 CPU at up to 40MHz. In October 1993, the Macintosh Centris series was merged into the Quadra series, bringing in some models with "pizza box" and horizontal desktop cases. The last member of the Quadra series was discontinued in October 1995.
