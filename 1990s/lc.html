The Macintosh LC was introduced in October 1990 as a low-cost entry-level Mac that could do color graphics (hence "LC" standing for "low cost" or "low-cost color"). The original LC had a 16MHz Motorola 68020 CPU, 2MB of RAM expandable to 10MB, and a choice of either one floppy drive and a 40MB or 80MB hard drive, or two floppy drives. This was all packaged in a "pizza box"-style flat desktop case. It sold for $2,499.

Several more models would follow the original; some used the "pizza box" case, while others were all-in-one desktops with built-in monitors. Only the original LC used the 68020 CPU; the rest used either the 68030, 68LC040, or PowerPC 603. The last model in the series was discontinued in early 1997.
