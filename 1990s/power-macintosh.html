The Power Macintosh series was introduced in March 1994. This was a series of high-end desktop computers using the then-new PowerPC CPU architecture, created by a partnership between Apple, IBM, and Motorola, which became Apple's new standard. As with the Macintosh Performa line, there were too many Power Macintosh models to individually mention on a single page.
The Power Macintosh line was comprised of horizontal, tower, and a few all-in-one desktop models featuring PowerPC 601, 603, 604, and later G3, G4, and G5 CPUs. They sold extremely well, with Apple selling selling a million Power Macintosh units by January 1995. The name was shortened to "Power Mac" in 1999, and the series was discontinued in 2006 with the switch to Intel CPUs.
