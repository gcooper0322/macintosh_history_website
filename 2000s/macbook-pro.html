The Intel-based MacBook Pro replaced the PowerBook G4 in February 2006. Its initial design was very similar to the aluminum models of the PowerBook G4, but it dropped the 12-inch variant, added an iSight camera above the display, and introduced the MagSafe power adapter port. It initially shipped with Core Duo CPUs, then Core 2 Duo soon after.

In October 2008, the MacBook Pro received its first redesign, featuring a "unibody" case milled from a single block of aluminum. This case was much more durable than the previous sheet-aluminum one, and was also slightly thinner. In addition, it introduced a new keyboard with black keys, sunk slightly into the case. In June 2009, a 13-inch variant was added to the MacBook Pro lineup. These variants remained mostly unchanged, apart from internal hardware upgrades, until 2012; the latest 13-inch variant with this design continued to be sold until October 2016.

In June 2012, a second MacBook Pro redesign was introduced that dropped the 17-inch variant, removed the internal DVD drive, and brought a new thinner unibody case, the slimmer MagSafe 2 power adapter port, solid-state storage as standard, and the first "Retina" display, with a resolution of 2880x1800 pixels. These "Retina" variants would continue to receive hardware updates until May 2015; the 15-inch variant continued to be sold until July 2018.

In October 2016, the third MacBook Pro redesign was revealed. This design introduced a new version of the unibody case that was even slimmer than that of the Retina variants, along with several new features: the Touch Bar (on all variants except the base 13-inch), a multi-touch-enabled display strip that took the place of the function keys at the top of the keyboard; all the ports were replaced with either two or four USB-C ports; and the trackpad was enlarged to take up all of the space between the bottom of the keyboard and the front of the computer. Unwelcome changes included the RAM and storage both being soldered to the mainboard, thus making them incapable of being replaced without advanced tools.
The "Touch Bar" variants also brought a few significant problems. The thin design, combined with powerful hardware such as the Core i9 CPU, caused constant overheating and thermal throttling. The then-new "butterfly" keyboard design was prone to failures such as stuck keys and unresponsive keys, often due to small particles getting stuck under the keys. Two class-action lawsuits were filed in May 2018 over the keyboard problem. Other user complaints included the absence of regular ports such as USB-A and HDMI, forcing the use of "dongle" adapters. Despite these numerous problems, the "Touch Bar" variants continued to be sold until 2021; a new 16-inch display size option was added in November 2019.

The first variant of the MacBook Pro to feature the then-new Apple M1 processor was introduced in November 2020. This initial M1 model was only available as a 13-inch; new 14-inch and 16-inch variants followed in October 2021, with the 15-inch option being dropped. The 14-inch and 16-inch variants introduced the MagSafe 3 power adapter port, and a new case that was slightly thicker and more squared-off than the last Intel models; its appearance has been said to be reminiscent of the titanium PowerBook G4 of 20 years earlier. These variants later received M2, M3, M4, and finally M5 hardware refreshes; they are still in production today.
