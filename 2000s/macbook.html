The MacBook was the Intel-based replacement for the iBook line, introduced in May 2006. It featured a polycarbonate plastic case in white or black, with a 13-inch widescreen display and many of the same new features as the MacBook Pro. As with the MacBook Pro, the MacBook initially shipped with Intel Core Duo CPUs, with the Core 2 Duo coming not long after. The black variant was dropped in October 2008, leaving only the white variant in production.

In October 2009, the MacBook received a redesign featuring a unibody case reminiscent of the MacBook Pro aluminum unibody, but still made of white polycarbonate plastic. It still featured Intel Core 2 Duo CPUs. This design was fairly short-lived, only receiving one minor hardware refresh before the model was discontinued in the consumer market in July 2011; it continued to be sold to educational institutions until February 2012.

The MacBook name was revived in April 2015 as an ultra-slim 12-inch laptop in a tapered unibody case similar to that of the MacBook Air. This new model featured low-power Intel Core m3, m5 or m7 CPUs to allow for a fanless design and a notably small mainboard. It was notably the first Mac to have USB-C on board, with the only port being a single USB-C port. This MacBook was criticized for being slower than the Air and Pro models, and it also had the "butterfly" keyboard, which later became infamous for key failures. It received two minor hardware refreshes, with the last of these occurring in June 2017, and was discontinued entirely in July 2019.
