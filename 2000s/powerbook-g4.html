The PowerBook G4 replaced the PowerBook G3 as Apple's professional laptop offering in January 2001. The initial variant of this model incorporated a number of innovations; notably, it was one of the first laptops to feature a widescreen display, and one of the thinnest laptops in the world at the time, at just one inch thick. It packed a 400MHz or 500MHz G4 CPU, 128MB or 256MB of RAM as standard, a 10GB, 20GB, or 30GB hard drive, a 6x speed DVD-ROM drive, a 15-inch widescreen display, and optional AirPort wireless networking, into a sleek gray-and-white laptop case made mostly of titanium. Due to the titanium case, it was unofficially nicknamed "TiBook". Incremental hardware revisions eventually brought CPU speeds up to a maximum of 1GHz.
The titanium variants were prone to some quality issues. A particularly glaring problem was that the display hinges were known to break off of the lid under typical use; Apple strengthened the hinges slightly in the middle of the production run, but this didn't help enough. Due to this and other, more minor issues, Apple opted to redesign the case entirely in 2003.

This redesign came in the form of the "aluminum" variants, the first of which was introduced in January 2003. In addition to a new thin aluminum construction and completely redesigned display hinge system, the redesign also introduced multiple screen sizes: 12-inch, 15-inch, and 17-inch. Other improvements included the introduction of faster DDR RAM, a new wireless networking card known as AirPort Extreme, and later, the world's first backlit laptop keyboard. CPU speeds for these variants started at 867MHz or 1GHz, and revisions brought these up to 1.5GHz or 1.67GHz. In 2006, these models were replaced by the MacBook Pro.
