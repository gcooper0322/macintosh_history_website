The Power Mac G4 replaced the Power Macintosh G3 as Apple's professional desktop offering in September 1999. The first variant packed the then-new PowerPC G4 CPU at 350MHz or 400MHz, 64MB of RAM, a 10GB hard drive, a 32x speed CD-ROM drive, and most of the same new I/O ports introduced with the iMac, into a case that was basically a gray version of the preceding G3's case.
The Power Mac G4 received several revisions that slightly changed the case style and brought many hardware improvements. By the end of its production run, it had gained dual-CPU variants, CPU speeds up to 1.42GHz, and up to 512MB of RAM as standard. It was fully replaced by the Power Mac G5 in June 2004.
