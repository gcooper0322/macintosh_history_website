The iMac G5 succeeded the iMac G4 in August 2004. It was not as radical a design as either of its predecessors, with a white plastic case looking essentially like a very thick LCD monitor unit, with all of the internal hardware positioned behind the display; this design would set the standard for all iMac models going forward. Initial variants used a single 1.6GHz or 1.8GHz G5 CPU; this was increased to 2.1GHz in the fastest variant. The iMac G5 had two display size options: 17-inch and 20-inch; a slot-loading DVD drive mounted in the right-hand side of the machine; AirPort Extreme and the then-new Bluetooth for wireless networking; and in later variants, an integrated "iSight" camera above the display.

In March 2006, the G5 CPU was replaced with the Intel Core Duo and then the Core 2 Duo, but the appearance of the machine remained largely the same. In September 2006, it gained a 24-inch display option. A new aluminum case in a very similar form factor was introduced in August 2007; relatively minor refreshes followed in October 2009 and October 2012, with the 2009 refresh also adding a 27-inch display option. Later, the iMac would gain a redesigned thinner case, though it otherwise looked similar to previous models, and impressive features such as "Retina" high-resolution displays.

The latest redesign of the iMac, introduced in April 2021, initially featured the Apple M1 processor; this was later upgraded to the M3, then the M4. This redesign brought the thinnest case yet, standardized on a 24-inch display rather than having multiple size options, and brought back a feature that hadn't been seen since 2001: multiple case colors to choose from. This is the version of the iMac that remains in production today.
