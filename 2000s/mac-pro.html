The Mac Pro was the Intel-based replacement for the Power Mac G5, introduced in August 2006. The case of the initial design was very similar to that of the G5; the biggest differences were that the Mac Pro had two CD drive bays, as opposed to the G5's single bay; and the ports and expansion slots on the back were arranged somewhat differently. This initial version featured one or two Intel Xeon CPUs at up to 3.2GHz, with up to 6 cores per CPU, and was produced until October 2013.

In December 2013, a rather striking refresh of the Mac Pro was introduced, in a small cylindrical form factor. In my personal opinion, this was quite an innovative design, but it had one big issue: it had none of the expandability of the previous design - no PCIe card slots, and no option for a second CPU. It was also significantly less easily serviceable. In addition, some users of the higher-end variants reported overheating problems; I get the feeling this was because Apple simply packed too much powerful hardware into too tight a space for proper cooling. This variant of the Mac Pro, nicknamed the "trash can", was produced with no changes until December 2019.

In June 2019, Apple went back to a tower design for a second refresh of the Mac Pro. This design was somewhat similar to the 2006-2012 variant, but featured a new front panel with a latticed design and no drive bays; the same lattice pattern was also employed in the rear panel. It was designed so that it could be used as a tower, or mounted in a server rack. This variant received an upgrade featuring one of Apple's own ARM-based processors, the M2 Ultra, in 2023.

The Mac Pro was finally discontinued in March 2026, with no direct successor; the Mac Studio is now the highest-end Mac desktop on offer.
