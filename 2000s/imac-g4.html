The iMac G4 was introduced in January 2002 as the successor to the original iMac. It wasn't nearly as colorful as the original iMac, but it made up for that with its own innovative design, which was inspired by a sunflower. Most of the hardware was in a dome-shaped enclosure; attached to the top of that dome was one end of a metal swiveling arm, and on the other end of that arm was a 15-inch LCD monitor. The initial variant had a 700MHz G4 CPU, 128MB or 256MB of RAM, a 40GB, 60GB, or 80GB hard drive, a CD-RW or DVD-ROM drive, and an optional AirPort card; later variants progressively brought the CPU speed up to 1.25GHz, added an AirPort Extreme card, and added 17-inch and 20-inch display options. The iMac G4 was discontinued in August 2004 in favor of its successor, the iMac G5.
