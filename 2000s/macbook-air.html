The MacBook Air was introduced in January 2008. It was initially advertised as "the world's thinnest laptop", at 0.75 inches thick, technically making it the first ultrabook; at its introduction, Apple CEO Steve Jobs pulled it out of a manila envelope as a demonstration of how thin it was. The first variant had a slim aluminum unibody case; 13-inch widescreen display similar to the plastic MacBook; a special low-power variant of the Intel Core 2 Duo CPU; and it was the first Mac laptop to have a solid-state storage option ($999 extra for a 64GB SSD!). The slower, but less expensive, storage option was an 80GB hard drive in the same tiny form factor as that of an iPod. The I/O ports in this initial design were contained in a small flip-down door that opened from the bottom of the computer's right side; this would later be changed to have the ports simply open in the side of the machine, like other MacBook models. This design received two minor hardware refreshes in October 2008 and June 2009.

In October 2010, the MacBook Air's first redesign was introduced, bringing a new tapered aluminum case, higher-resolution display, and standard solid-state storage. A new variant with an 11-inch screen was also introduced; this was discontinued in 2017. These variants received several minor hardware refreshes, with the last of these occurring in 2017; sales of this design were discontinued in July 2019.

A second redesign followed in October 2018, bringing a thinner case and Retina displays while replacing all of the ports with two USB-C ports. Two minor hardware refreshes occurred in 2019 and 2020; the Intel model was discontinued in November 2020, while a variant with the Apple M1 was introduced nearly simultaneously.

In June 2022, the third redesign of the MacBook Air was introduced, featuring the Apple M2 processor and 13-inch display in a slim case shaped similarly to that of the 2021 MacBook Pro models. A 15-inch variant was introduced in June 2023. These variants have received M3 and M4 hardware refreshes, and currently remain in production.
