The Mac Mini was introduced in January 2005 as a small-form-factor desktop, originally featuring the PowerPC G4 CPU. Selling for just $499, it was designed as an inexpensive option for PC users wanting to switch to a Mac, and to that end, didn't ship with a monitor, keyboard, or mouse, instead giving said PC users the option to use their existing ones. It was housed in a small rectangular aluminum-and-plastic case - 6.5 inches wide, 6.5 inches deep, and 2 inches tall - with a slot-loading DVD drive in the front and an external power adapter.

Barely a year after the model's introduction, in February 2006, Apple introduced a version of the Mac Mini featuring Intel Core CPUs. This initial Intel variant was externally nearly the same as the PowerPC variant, with minimal visual differences. In June 2010, the model's first major redesign gave it a thinner "unibody" aluminum case - 1.4 inches tall, 7.7 inches in the other dimensions - and an internal power supply, and in 2011, the DVD drive slot was removed from the design. This case design was used up through the "Apple silicon" transition, with the last variants featuring M2 chips.

In October 2024, the Mac Mini received its second major redesign, introducing a significantly smaller case - 2 inches tall, 5 inches in other dimensions - and M4 processors. This second redesign is currently still in production.
